What the RFEOI asks for, in our words.

You asked for learning that fits northern lodge life

The RFEOI describes remote communities, limited internet and busy seasonal staff. It asks for short modules, downloadable tools, visual guides, checklists and optional live sessions, with links to existing certifications where they already cover a subject.

Start from the Toolkit

The Partnership Toolkit is the source. The pilot lodges helped shape it, so the training carries their standards forward in a form new staff can learn from.

Ask the lodges first

The Governing Body and the four pilot lodges confirm who the learners are and which topics come first, before any module is written.

Respect what belongs to each Nation

The work aligns with the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples and BC's Declaration Act. Stories, protocols and cultural knowledge stay with the Nations and lodges that share them.

Link to what exists

Where a recognized certification already teaches a subject, the module links to it and spends its time on what only NILN can teach.

Training that people use and complete.

Junction has spent more than a decade developing training programs for tourism operators, and 95% of learners who start our Accelerator program finish it. Try a module
How a lesson could look inside ITBC's LMS, on a computer and on an iPhone. Press play, answer the quick check, tick the checklist.

What one module looks like to a new hire

The program will be built in the learning platform ITBC already has. Our contribution is the training inside it. This sample shows the format we propose for every NILN module: lessons of ten minutes or less, a quick check, a checklist to print and post, a visual guide, links to existing training and a short feedback form.

The same module on an iPhone

Most lodge staff will open training on a phone, so the phone layout is designed first. The two views here are in sync. Tap through either one and the other follows.

  • One column and one action per screen, with the main button where a thumb reaches it.
  • Captions on every video, so a lesson works with the sound off.
  • A low-bandwidth switch that swaps the video for text.
  • A quick check and a checklist in every lesson, each answered in a tap.

This module and Conner's Cabins are invented to show the format, and the final look follows ITBC's LMS and the NILN brand. The sample leaves out cultural protocol content on purpose. In the real program that content, and the wording of every welcome, comes from each lodge and the NILN Governing Body.

For the lodge team

A seasonal hire can start on any day and finish a lesson on a break. The checklist goes on the wall, so the standard stays in view after the lesson ends.

For the Governing Body

Every module follows the same structure, so new topics can be added later by NILN or any provider. Feedback from each module reports against the 80% target.

Module topics
Open a topic to see what a lodge leaves with.

The topics in the RFEOI, and how we would treat each

The RFEOI names nine topic areas. We have grouped them into six starting points. The Governing Body and the pilot lodges rank them, and the training framework confirms the final 6 to 8 modules.

Five weeks in December is a short window. We build modules in the order the Governing Body ranks them. Any module that cannot be built and reviewed properly by December 18 is delivered fully scoped: learning outcomes, lesson outline, tool list and linked certifications, ready to build.

Platform
Where the training lives, and what we ask of ITBC.

Built in the learning platform ITBC already has

Junction and Tourismo deliver TIAC's Digital Tourism READI together. Tourismo provides the course infrastructure and Junction leads the curriculum. We understand through that partnership that ITBC already holds a licence for Tourismo's LMS, so NILN's program can be built there with no software to buy.

Software cost: $0

With no platform fee, the largest line in our quotation goes to modules, tools and checklists.

A recommendation you can check

The RFEOI asks for a platform recommendation. ITBC's existing Tourismo LMS is our starting assumption. The training framework tests it against what the lodges tell us about bandwidth and devices before we recommend it.

Our partnership, stated plainly

Junction and Tourismo are delivery partners on Digital Tourism READI. We name the relationship here so ITBC can weigh our platform advice with that in view.
